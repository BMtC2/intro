The work

Not a tool service. A judgement question, asked one assessment at a time.

The BEL Graduate AI Initiative is a five-year, philanthropically funded program building AI capability across the Bachelor of Business Management core, Year 1 through Year 3. It is governed by a Steering Committee chaired by Rachel Fitzgerald.

My part is the integration. For any given task, the question is what thinking it is meant to build, and whether AI would sharpen that thinking or quietly do it instead. Where AI helps, students learn to use it well. Where it would replace the learning, it stays out, and students are told why.

Three things come out the other side: a better learning experience, sharper critical judgement, and students who can use AI responsibly because they have practised it inside their own discipline rather than been warned about it in the abstract.

How it happens

Four steps, and a floor underneath them.

01

Think

Read the course. Find what each task is actually meant to evidence, before anyone mentions a tool.

02

Co-design

Sit with the coordinator. They know the discipline; I know what AI does and does not do well. The decision is joint.

03

Design and validate

Build the thing, then test it against real failure cases before a student ever sees it.

04

Deploy

Into the course, on the course's own page. The coordinator owns it from that point.

The floor is consistency and stability. Every course gets the same structure, the same interface and the same standard, so a student who meets one has met all of them. Nothing depends on a login, a network call or a service that might not be there next semester.

Why it is not guesswork

We asked 138 first-year students what they actually do, before we built anything else.

Of the students who told us they rarely check what AI gives them, most were not using it to find sources at all. They were asking it to explain things, and believing the explanation.

So we stopped teaching them to check citations and started teaching them to check claims. A second survey at the end of semester will tell us whether that change did anything. That comparison is the only real evidence any of this works, and it is the piece I care most about.

Also

No student data is collected anywhere it can be avoided. The modules run entirely in the browser and store nothing. Where a tool does need to hold something, it stays inside University systems, and I would rather raise it with you first than have you find out afterwards.

The coaches are built on Copilot Studio, so they sit on tooling the University already has. Nothing is locked to it, and the same design would move to another model if that ever made sense.

Customisation and student experience come first. Every course gets its own version rather than a shared page, because a generic one teaches nobody. Happy to talk to any academic who wants to look at what this could do in their course.
